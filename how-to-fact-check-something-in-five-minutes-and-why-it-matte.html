How to fact-check something in five minutes (and why it matters)

When you spend time online, the difference between a page that helps and one that wastes your time comes down to a few simple signals.

  • Cross-reference with other sources: Look for the same information in other reputable sources. If multiple credible sources are reporting the same thing, it’s more likely to be accurate.
  • Use fact-checking websites: Websites like Snopes, FactCheck.org, and PolitiFact are dedicated to verifying claims and debunking misinformation. A quick search on these sites can provide clarity.
  • Check for primary sources: If the information claims to be based on a study, report, or document, try to discover the primary source. This allows you to see the original data or context.

Reliable sources typically include well-known news organizations, academic institutions, and government websites. If the source is unfamiliar, take a moment to research it. A quick search can reveal whether the source is known for credible information or if it has a history of spreading misinformation.

The first step in fact-checking is to identify the source of the information. Ask yourself the following questions:

  • Does the source have a particular political or ideological leaning?
  • Is the language used in the article or post neutral, or does it seem to be pushing a specific agenda?
  • Are multiple perspectives presented, or is only one side of the story being told?
