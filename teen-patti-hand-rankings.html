Understanding Teen Patti Hand Rankings: A Comprehensive Guide

Teen Patti, also known as Indian Poker, is a popular card game that has been enjoyed by millions across South Asia and beyond. Whether you're a seasoned player or a newcomer, understanding the hand rankings in Teen Patti is crucial for mastering the game. This guide will walk you through the various hand rankings, helping you make informed decisions and improve your gameplay.

1. Trio (Trail or Set)

The highest-ranking hand in Teen Patti is the Trio, also known as a Trail or Set. A Trio consists of three cards of the same rank, regardless of the suit. For example, three Aces, three Kings, or three 5s form a Trio. Here’s a breakdown of how Trios are ranked:

  • Three Aces - The highest possible Trio.
  • Three Kings
  • Three Queens
  • Three Jacks
  • And so on, down to three 2s, which is the lowest Trio.

Note that the rank of the Trio is determined by the rank of the cards, not the suits.

2. Pure Sequence (Straight Flush)

The second-highest hand in Teen Patti is the Pure Sequence, also known as a Straight Flush. This hand consists of three consecutive cards of the same suit. For example, 4♥, 5♥, 6♥ is a Pure Sequence. Here’s how Pure Sequences are ranked:

  • A, K, Q of the same suit - The highest possible Pure Sequence.
  • K, Q, J of the same suit
  • Q, J, 10 of the same suit
  • And so on, down to 2, 3, 4 of the same suit, which is the lowest Pure Sequence.

Remember, the Ace can be used as the highest or lowest card in a Pure Sequence, but not both at the same time. For example, A, 2, 3 is a valid Pure Sequence, but Q, K, A is also valid.

3. Sequence (Straight)

A Sequence, or Straight, is similar to a Pure Sequence but with one key difference: the cards do not need to be of the same suit. For example, 7♠, 8♥, 9♦ is a Sequence. The ranking of Sequences is the same as for Pure Sequences:

  • A, K, Q - The highest possible Sequence.
  • K, Q, J
  • Q, J, 10
  • And so on, down to 2, 3, 4, which is the lowest Sequence.

Again, the Ace can be used as the highest or lowest card, but not both.

4. Color (Flush)

A Color, or Flush, consists of three cards of the same suit, but they do not need to be in sequence. For example, 3♣, 6♣, 9♣ is a Color. If two players have a Color, the one with the highest card wins. If the highest cards are the same, the second highest card is compared, and so on.

5. Pair (Two of a Kind)

A Pair consists of two cards of the same rank, with the third card being of a different rank. For example, 10♥, 10♦, 4♠ is a Pair. If two players have a Pair, the higher pair wins. If the pairs are of the same rank, the highest third card determines the winner.

6. High Card

If none of the above hands are present, the highest card in the hand is considered. For example, if you have 5♠, 9♥, K♦, the highest card is the King. If two players have the same high card, the next highest card is compared, and so on.
