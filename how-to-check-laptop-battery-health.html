How To Check Laptop Battery Health

Checking laptop battery health is useful when your battery drains faster than expected, your charger cannot keep up, or the machine shuts down unexpectedly. The quickest method depends on your operating system, although manufacturer diagnostic tools can provide the most relevant information for your particular laptop.

For more on this, see how to check laptop battery health.

What battery health actually means

Battery health is an estimate of how much charge the battery can still hold compared with when it was new. It is usually based on the battery’s maximum or full-charge capacity, its original design capacity, and sometimes its charge-cycle count. A battery may still power your laptop for several hours, but a reduced health percentage can indicate that its runtime and peak performance will continue to decline.

Check battery health on Windows

Windows includes a built-in battery report that is more informative than the percentage shown in Settings.

  • Open Command Prompt. You can search for it from the Start menu and choose Run as administrator if prompted.
  • Enter powercfg /batteryreport and press Enter.
  • Open the generated battery-report.html file. It is normally saved in your user folder, such as C:\Users\YourName.
  • Review the Batteries section. Look for design capacity, full charge capacity, cycle count, and recent battery usage. Not every computer displays every field.

To estimate wear, divide full charge capacity by design capacity and multiply by 100. For example, a battery with 80 percent of its original full-charge capacity has approximately 20 percent wear. The calculation is an estimate, so compare the result with the manufacturer’s guidance for your model.

Check battery health on macOS

On a MacBook, look for battery information in the built-in system report:

  • Open the Apple menu, choose About This System, and then open System Report.
  • Select Power in the sidebar.
  • Review the battery condition, cycle count, charge capacity, and other available details. The exact labels vary by macOS version and hardware.

Some recent Mac models also show a battery-health notification in System Settings or under About This System. If macOS recommends service, follow that recommendation or contact Apple support.

Check battery health on Linux

Linux users can try the power utility:

  • Run upower -e to list connected batteries.
  • Run upower -i /BAT0, replacing BAT0 with the identifier listed by the first command.
  • Look for the current percentage, energy values, state, and cycle information. The available fields depend on the system and battery driver.

Use your manufacturer’s diagnostic tool

The laptop maker’s support application, BIOS, or customer-service diagnostic page may provide battery-health information, estimated remaining life, and model-specific tests. This is often the best source when you need to know whether a reading is normal for your computer. Search the manufacturer’s support site using your exact model number, and avoid third-party “battery checker” websites that request unnecessary personal information.

How to interpret the results

  • Capacity: A lower full-charge capacity than design capacity indicates wear.
  • Cycle count: Many charges add up gradually, but cycle count alone does not determine battery condition.
  • Rapid changes: A sudden decline can result from age, heat, a damaged cell, or a calibration issue.
  • Runtime: Compare actual use with the battery percentage. A healthy-looking battery that drops quickly may have high background power use or an application consuming excessive resources.

When to replace or service the battery

Consider professional service if capacity loss is affecting your work, the battery reports an internal error, charging behaves inconsistently, or the laptop shuts down while still above a low battery level. Stop using the battery immediately if it becomes swollen, develops an unusual odor, leaks, or is physically damaged. Do not puncture or open a battery cell.

Extend battery life with simple habits

  • Keep the laptop cool and avoid leaving it in hot cars or direct sunlight.
  • Use the charger that came with the laptop or a manufacturer-approved replacement.
  • Avoid keeping the battery at zero or full charge for long periods; moderate charge levels are generally kinder.
  • Update the operating system and drivers, and close unnecessary programs that drain power.

For the most accurate assessment, check the built-in report, compare the reading with manufacturer guidance, and treat battery replacement as a likely solution when capacity loss is substantial rather than relying on cycle count alone.
