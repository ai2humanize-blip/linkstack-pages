How to Recognize High-Quality Seafood

Freshness affects flavor, texture, shelf life, and safety. Rather than relying on appearance alone, consider the condition of the seafood, the way it is stored, and whether the business provides clear information about its origin and handling.

  • Inspect whole fish carefully. Whole fish is often easier to evaluate than fillets. Look for clear, shiny skin, firm flesh, and a mild marine smell. Very cloudy eyes, excessive slime, or a strong odor are reasons to move on.
  • Check fillets for color and texture. Fillets should look moist rather than dry, with natural color and no discoloration around the edges. Severe browning, drying, or an unusual odor can indicate age or poor handling.
  • Examine shellfish. Live crabs and other live shellfish should move when touched. Shucked oysters and clams should have clear liquor, firm flesh, and a fresh smell. Keep the shells closed or slightly open when properly chilled.
  • Ask about storage and handling. Seafood should be kept cold throughout display, transport, and preparation. Clean counters, organized cases, and knowledgeable staff are useful signs of careful service.

Fresh, Frozen, and Previously Frozen Seafood

Fresh does not always mean local, and frozen does not automatically mean inferior. High-quality frozen seafood is often flash-frozen shortly after harvest, preserving flavor and texture well. Previously frozen fillets can be an excellent option when they have been thawed properly and handled with care.

When shopping through Sea Breeze Seafood, ask whether a product is fresh, frozen, or previously frozen if that information is not already displayed. This matters for people following specific recipes, avoiding certain textures, or planning meals in advance. It also helps set realistic expectations about cooking time and final moisture.

Popular Seafood Choices and Their Best Uses

  • Salmon is versatile and works well roasted, grilled, pan-seared, or used in pasta and grain bowls. Its rich flavor pairs easily with herbs, citrus, mustard, and roasted vegetables.
  • White fish such as cod, pollock, haddock, or flounder generally has a mild flavor and firm texture. It is a practical choice for tacos, fish sandwiches, steaming, and frying.
  • Tuna can suit quick-cooked dishes or richer preparations, depending on the cut. Ask about the species and whether the product is suitable for the cooking method you have in mind.
  • Shellfish brings sweetness and texture to soups, stews, pasta, and grilled meals. Mussels, clams, shrimp, scallops, crabs, and lobster can each replace one another, but cooking times and flavor profiles vary.

Handling Seafood Safely at Home

Raw seafood should be refrigerated promptly at 40°F/4°C or below and kept separate from ready-to-eat foods. Use clean cutting boards, wash hands and utensils after handling raw products, and cook seafood to a safe internal temperature. Follow package instructions and any use-by guidance provided by the seller.

If you are not cooking seafood immediately, ask whether freezing is appropriate. Freeze portions that will not be used within the recommended refrigerator window, and thaw them in the refrigerator rather than on the counter.
