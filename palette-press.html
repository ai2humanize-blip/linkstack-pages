Palette Press

A Practical Guide to Choosing Fine-Art Prints Through Palette Press

Ordering through Palette Press is about more than moving an image from a screen to a wall. The finished piece depends on the source file, printing method, surface, color handling, scale, framing, and location. A useful print partner should explain those choices clearly rather than treating them as interchangeable. Since products and policies can change, confirm current materials, production times, and correction terms for the specific order.

Start with the strongest available image

A print cannot reliably restore detail that was never captured. Begin with the highest-quality digital file or the best scan available, and ask how its pixel dimensions relate to the intended print size. This matters because enlarging an image too aggressively can reveal softness, pixelation, noise, or compression artifacts. Avoid using a heavily compressed file from a messaging app when an original is available.

  • Define the crop: Decide whether the full composition or a tighter detail should appear.
  • Check the edges: Leave enough room for cropping without accidentally cutting off important content.
  • Keep edits controlled: Basic tonal or color adjustments can help, but heavy filters may conceal problems that become visible in print.

Confirm that any reproduction, licensing, or sales permission is in place before the print is produced or sold.

Choose a surface based on the work, not the trend

Paper, canvas, metal, and other substrates create very different results. The right choice should support the artwork and suit the setting.

  • Paper: Often suited to drawings, photographs, and quieter displays. It can offer a broad tonal range and a softer, more traditional presentation, with matte or gloss finishes affecting reflection.
  • Canvas: Useful when texture should become part of the image or when a photograph needs a more substantial presence. The weave can also influence fine details.
  • Metal: Can suit bold photography, graphic compositions, and contemporary interiors. Review a sample if possible, because reflective surfaces and contrast can alter the perceived color.
  • Framed works: A frame and mat can complete the presentation, but they should be selected after the image and print format are settled.
