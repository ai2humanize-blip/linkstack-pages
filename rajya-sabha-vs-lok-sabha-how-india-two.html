Understanding the Two Houses of Indian Parliament: Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha

India, the world's largest democracy, has a bicameral parliamentary system consisting of two Houses: the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). Both Houses play crucial roles in the law-making process and are vital for the functioning of the Indian democracy. This article aims to elucidate the key differences and functions of these two important institutions.

What is the Lok Sabha?

The Lok Sabha is the lower house of India's Parliament and is often considered the more powerful of the two Houses. Here are some key features of the Lok Sabha:

  • Composition: The Lok Sabha consists of 545 members, out of which 543 are directly elected by the people of India through a general election held every five years, unless dissolved earlier. The remaining two members are nominated by the President of India to represent the Anglo-Indian community.
  • Role and Functions: The primary function of the Lok Sabha is to legislate and make laws. It has the power to introduce and pass bills, including money bills, which are crucial for the government's financial policies. The Lok Sabha also holds the executive accountable through question hours, debates, and motions of no confidence.
  • Speaker: The presiding officer of the Lok Sabha is the Speaker, who is elected by its members. The Speaker plays a crucial role in maintaining order and decorum during debates and ensuring that the House's business is conducted smoothly.
  • Special Powers: The Lok Sabha has the exclusive power to introduce and pass money bills. It also has the final say in the event of a disagreement with the Rajya Sabha on ordinary bills, provided that the Rajya Sabha's suggestions are not accepted by the Lok Sabha.

What is the Rajya Sabha?

The Rajya Sabha is the upper house of India's Parliament and is designed to represent the states of the Indian Union. Here are some key features of the Rajya Sabha:

  • Composition: The Rajya Sabha consists of 250 members, out of which 238 are representatives of the states and Union territories, elected by the members of State Legislative Assemblies. The remaining 12 members are nominated by the President of India for their contributions to art, literature, science, and social services.
  • Role and Functions: The Rajya Sabha plays a significant role in the law-making process. It can introduce, amend, and pass bills, except for money bills, which originate in the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha acts as a revising chamber, scrutinizing and improving legislation passed by the Lok Sabha.
  • Chairman: The Vice President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The Chairman presides over the House's proceedings and maintains order during debates.
  • Special Powers: The Rajya Sabha has some unique powers, such as the ability to create or abolish any All-India Service. It also plays a crucial role in the impeachment of the President, the removal of judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, and the approval of emergency proclamations.

Key Differences Between Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha

While both Houses are integral to the functioning of India's democracy, there are several key differences between them:

  • Electoral Process: Members of the Lok Sabha are directly elected by the people, whereas members of the Rajya Sabha are elected by the members of State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Term: The term of the Lok Sabha is five years, unless dissolved earlier, while the Rajya Sabha is a permanent body with one-third of its members retiring every two years.
  • Power Over Money Bills: The Lok Sabha has the exclusive power to introduce and pass money bills, while the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations, which the Lok Sabha can choose to accept or reject.
  • Representation: The Lok Sabha represents the people of India, while the Rajya Sabha represents the states and Union territories.

Conclusion

Both the Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha are essential components of India's parliamentary system. While the Lok Sabha is the more powerful of the two in terms of legislative authority, the Rajya Sabha serves as a vital check and balance, ensuring that the interests of the states and the nation as a whole are represented and protected. Understanding the roles and functions of these two Houses is crucial for anyone wishing to comprehend the intricacies of Indian democracy.
