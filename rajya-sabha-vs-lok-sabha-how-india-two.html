Understanding the Two Houses of Indian Parliament: Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha

India, the world's largest democracy, has a bicameral parliamentary system consisting of two Houses: the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). Both Houses play crucial roles in the law-making process and governance of the country. However, they differ in their composition, powers, and functions. This article aims to elucidate the distinctions and similarities between the Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha, providing a comprehensive understanding of how India's two Houses operate.

Composition and Membership

Lok Sabha: The Lok Sabha is the lower house of the Indian Parliament and is composed of members directly elected by the people. As of now, the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 552 members, out of which 530 represent the states, 20 represent the Union Territories, and 2 members are nominated by the President of India to represent the Anglo-Indian community, if deemed necessary. The term of the Lok Sabha is five years, unless dissolved earlier.

Rajya Sabha: The Rajya Sabha is the upper house and represents the states of the Indian Union. It has a maximum of 250 members. Out of these, 238 members are elected by the Legislative Assemblies of the states and Union Territories through a single transferable vote system. The remaining 12 members are nominated by the President of India for their contributions to art, literature, science, and social services. Unlike the Lok Sabha, the Rajya Sabha is a permanent body and is not subject to dissolution. However, one-third of its members retire every two years, ensuring continuity.

Functions and Powers

Both Houses of Parliament have distinct roles and responsibilities, although they share some common functions:

  • Legislative Functions: The primary function of both Houses is to make laws. Bills can originate in either House, except for Money Bills, which can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha can suggest amendments to Money Bills, but the Lok Sabha has the final say.
  • Financial Functions: The Lok Sabha holds greater financial powers. It is responsible for the passing of the annual budget and all Money Bills. The Rajya Sabha can discuss and review these Bills but cannot amend or reject them.
  • Executive Functions: The Lok Sabha exercises control over the executive branch through various mechanisms, such as questioning government policies, moving adjournment motions, and impeaching the President. The Rajya Sabha also plays a role in these functions but to a lesser extent.
  • Electoral Functions: Both Houses participate in the election of the President and Vice President of India. The Rajya Sabha, however, has a more significant role in the election of the Vice President.
  • Amendment of the Constitution: Amendments to the Constitution of India require the approval of both Houses. In this regard, the Rajya Sabha has equal powers with the Lok Sabha.

Special Powers of the Rajya Sabha

While the Lok Sabha is more powerful in certain areas, the Rajya Sabha has some unique powers:

  • Power to Create All-India Services: The Rajya Sabha can pass a resolution, by a two-thirds majority, to create new All-India Services that are common to both the Union and the states.
  • Power to Discuss and Debate: The Rajya Sabha often serves as a platform for in-depth discussion and debate on national issues. Its members, being representatives of the states, bring diverse perspectives to the table.
  • Role in Impeachment Proceedings: The Rajya Sabha has a role in the impeachment of certain high constitutional authorities, such as the Comptroller and Auditor General of India and the Chief Election Commissioner.

Conclusion

The Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha, though distinct in their composition and certain powers, work in tandem to uphold the democratic principles of the Indian Constitution. The Lok Sabha, with its direct representation of the people, ensures that the voice of the nation is heard, while the Rajya Sabha, representing the states, provides a federal balance and acts as a check on hasty legislation. Together, they form the cornerstone of India's parliamentary democracy, facilitating a system of checks and balances that is essential for a thriving democracy.

Understanding the roles and responsibilities of these two Houses is crucial for every citizen, as it fosters informed participation in the democratic process and promotes a deeper appreciation of India's constitutional framework.
