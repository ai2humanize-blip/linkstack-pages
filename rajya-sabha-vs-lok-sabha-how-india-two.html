Understanding the Two Houses of Indian Parliament: Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha

India, the world's largest democracy, has a bicameral parliamentary system consisting of two Houses: the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). Both Houses play crucial roles in the law-making process and governance of the country. However, they differ in their composition, powers, and functions. This article aims to elucidate the distinctions and similarities between the Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha.

Composition and Membership

Lok Sabha:

  • The Lok Sabha is the lower house of the Indian Parliament and is composed of members directly elected by the people.
  • As per the Constitution of India, the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 552 members, out of which 530 represent the states, 20 represent the Union Territories, and 2 members are nominated by the President of India to represent the Anglo-Indian community.
  • The term of the Lok Sabha is five years, unless dissolved earlier.

Rajya Sabha:

  • The Rajya Sabha is the upper house and represents the states of the Indian Union.
  • It has a maximum of 250 members, out of which 238 are representatives of the states and Union Territories, elected by the members of the respective state legislative assemblies. The remaining 12 members are nominated by the President of India for their contributions to art, literature, science, and social services.
  • The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body and is not subject to dissolution. However, one-third of its members retire every two years, ensuring continuity.

Powers and Functions

Legislative Powers:

  • Both Houses have equal legislative powers, except in the case of money bills. A money bill can only originate in the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations, which the Lok Sabha may or may not accept.
  • All other bills can be introduced in either House. However, a bill must be passed by both Houses to become law. In case of a disagreement, a joint session of both Houses is called, where the will of the Lok Sabha often prevails due to its larger membership.

Financial Powers:

  • The Lok Sabha holds greater financial powers. It is responsible for the passing of the annual budget and financial legislation.
  • The Rajya Sabha can discuss and make recommendations on financial matters, but it cannot amend or reject a money bill.

Executive Powers:

  • The executive is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. The Council of Ministers, led by the Prime Minister, is answerable to the Lok Sabha and must resign if it loses the confidence of the House.
  • The Rajya Sabha does not have the power to remove the Council of Ministers through a no-confidence motion. However, it can influence the executive through discussions, debates, and by asking questions.

Other Functions:

  • Both Houses participate in the process of amending the Constitution. A constitutional amendment bill must be passed by both Houses with a special majority.
  • The Rajya Sabha has a specific role in matters relating to the creation and abolition of states, as well as in the election of the Vice President and the impeachment of the President and the judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • The Lok Sabha, being directly elected by the people, reflects the popular will and plays a significant role in representing the citizens' interests.

Key Differences

While both Houses are integral to the functioning of the Indian Parliament, there are some key differences:

  • Electoral Process: Members of the Lok Sabha are directly elected by the people, whereas members of the Rajya Sabha are elected by the members of the state legislative assemblies.
  • Term: The Lok Sabha has a fixed term of five years, while the Rajya Sabha is a permanent body with one-third of its members retiring every two years.
  • Representation: The Lok Sabha represents the people of India, while the Rajya Sabha represents the states.
  • Financial Control: The Lok Sabha has superior financial powers, including the passage of money bills.

Conclusion

The Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha, though distinct in their composition and certain powers, work in tandem to ensure democratic governance in India. The Lok Sabha, with its direct representation, brings the voice of the people into the legislative process, while the Rajya Sabha, representing the states, ensures that regional interests are safeguarded. Together, they form the cornerstone of India's parliamentary democracy, upholding the principles of federalism, representation, and accountability.
