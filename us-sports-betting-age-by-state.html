Understanding US Sports Betting Age by State

Sports betting has seen a significant transformation in the United States over recent years. With the repeal of the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act (PASPA) in 2018, individual states have been granted the authority to legalize and regulate sports betting as they see fit. However, one of the most crucial aspects of sports betting regulation is the age requirement, which varies from state to state. This article provides a comprehensive overview of the sports betting age requirements across different states in the US.

General Overview of Sports Betting Age in the US

In the United States, the legal age for gambling, including sports betting, is primarily determined by individual states. While most states have set the minimum age at 21, some have opted for 18. The reasoning behind these age restrictions is to protect minors from the potential harms associated with gambling and to ensure that individuals engaging in betting activities are legally adults.

Here is a breakdown of the general age requirements for sports betting in the US:

  • 21 Years Old: The majority of states that have legalized sports betting require individuals to be at least 21 years old to participate.
  • 18 Years Old: A smaller number of states allow individuals who are 18 and older to engage in sports betting.

States Where the Legal Age for Sports Betting is 21

Most states that have legalized sports betting have set the minimum age at 21. This includes:

  • Nevada: As the pioneer of modern sports betting in the US, Nevada has long required individuals to be 21 to place bets.
  • New Jersey: Following the repeal of PASPA, New Jersey quickly legalized sports betting with a minimum age of 21.
  • Pennsylvania: The Keystone State also mandates that bettors be at least 21 years old.
  • Illinois: Sports betting is legal for those 21 and older in Illinois.
  • Colorado: Colorado's sports betting laws require individuals to be at least 21.
  • New York: As of 2021, New York joined the list of states requiring bettors to be 21.
  • Michigan: Michigan's sports betting regulations also set the age at 21.

Other states with a 21-year-old minimum age include Delaware, Iowa, Rhode Island, and West Virginia.

States Where the Legal Age for Sports Betting is 18

While the majority of states have set the age at 21, some have opted for a lower age requirement. These states include:

  • Wyoming: Wyoming allows individuals who are 18 and older to participate in sports betting.
  • New Hampshire: The Granite State permits sports betting for those aged 18 and up.
  • Montana: Montana's sports betting laws also set the minimum age at 18.
  • Washington, D.C.: The nation's capital allows 18-year-olds to engage in sports betting.

It's important to note that while these states allow 18-year-olds to bet on sports, some operators or specific platforms may still require individuals to be 21.

Additional Considerations

When it comes to sports betting, age is not the only factor to consider. Here are a few additional points to keep in mind:

  • Online vs. In-Person Betting: Some states may have different age requirements for online sports betting compared to in-person betting at physical locations like casinos or sportsbooks.
  • Identity Verification: Sportsbooks and betting platforms are required to verify the age and identity of their users. This is to ensure compliance with state regulations and to prevent underage gambling.
  • Local Laws: Always check the specific laws and regulations in your state, as they can change. State legislatures are continually reviewing and updating their gambling laws.

In conclusion, the legal age for sports betting in the US varies by state, with the majority setting the minimum age at 21. However, a few states allow 18-year-olds to participate. Understanding these age requirements is crucial for anyone looking to engage in sports betting legally and responsibly.
