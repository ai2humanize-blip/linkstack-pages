How To Check Tire Pressure

How to Check Tire Pressure Safely and Accurately

Checking tire pressure is a simple task, but doing it correctly helps your tires grip the road, wear evenly, and work as intended. You can check all four tires at home with a tire-pressure gauge or use the gauge at a service station. The most important part is knowing which pressure your vehicle requires and when to measure it.

Find the Right Target Pressure

The best source is the tire-information placard inside your vehicle, usually on the driver’s door jamb or the door frame. It lists the recommended cold inflation pressure for the front and rear tires. Your owner’s manual may provide the same information. Use the vehicle placard because tire sizes and configurations can differ between models.

Do not use the maximum pressure printed on the tire sidewall as your target. That number is a safety limit, not the recommended operating pressure. It also may apply to a different load rating or use. If the vehicle placard specifies different front and rear pressures, follow those values. Some placards include alternate pressures for heavy loads; never exceed the tire’s permitted maximum.

Check the Tires When They Are Cold

Measure pressure before driving far or after the vehicle has been parked long enough for the tires to cool. Air pressure rises as tire temperature rises, so a warm tire can read several units higher than its cold pressure. Do not release air from a warm tire merely to match a cold-pressure number, or the tire may be underinflated once it cools.

If you must check warm tires, note the temperature and check them again when cold. Warm readings are still useful for spotting a tire that is substantially lower than the others.

How to Check Each Tire

  • Park on level, safe ground. Set the parking brake and place wheel chocks if the vehicle could move. Check the tires before loading the vehicle, or follow the placard’s guidance for loaded weight.
  • Remove the valve cap. Keep the cap if it protects the valve from dirt and moisture; it does not hold air pressure inside the tire.
  • Read the gauge correctly. Press the gauge firmly onto the valve stem until it seals, then hold it steady. A pencil-style gauge is pressed into the stem; a digital gauge gives a direct reading.
  • Measure every tire. Include the spare if it is part of the vehicle’s equipment and will be used. Also inspect the spare for age, damage, and correct pressure.
  • Compare each reading with the placard. Pay particular attention to a tire that differs significantly from the others. A single low reading may indicate a leak or damage.
  • Add air as needed. If the tire is below the recommended pressure, add air in small amounts and check again. Avoid repeatedly pressing the gauge against a valve core, which can damage it or let air escape.
  • Recheck after adjustment. Some gauges bleed a small amount of air while connecting, so verify the final pressure. Replace the valve cap when finished.

Use the TPMS as a Backup, Not a Substitute

A tire-pressure monitoring system can warn you when a tire’s pressure falls below a specified threshold. Treat that warning as a prompt to inspect and check the tire promptly. The warning may not identify every slightly underinflated tire, and a system can also fail without displaying a warning. Manual checks remain necessary because tread contact, fuel efficiency, and tire condition can be affected before the warning appears.

Know When a Low Reading Means More Than a Flat Tire

Air loss can result from a puncture, a damaged valve stem, a wheel or rim leak, or a separated tire bead. Inspect the tire for cuts, bulges, embedded objects, and tread damage. Do not continue normal driving with a severely low tire, visible sidewall damage, or a tire that repeatedly loses pressure. If you find a nail or screw in the tread, follow your vehicle’s repair guidance rather than automatically removing it.

Check pressure periodically, before long trips, and whenever you notice handling changes, unusual tire wear, or a TPMS warning. If a tire repeatedly needs air, have a qualified tire professional inspect it, including the interior for hidden damage and the wheel for air loss. Keeping a reliable gauge in your vehicle makes regular checks quick and straightforward.
