How To Check Tire Pressure

How to Check Tire Pressure: A Simple, Step-by-Step Guide

Checking tire pressure is a quick way to help your tires grip, brake, and wear as intended. The key is comparing each tire with the vehicle manufacturer’s recommended pressure, not with the maximum printed on the tire. Check at least monthly, before long trips, and whenever a tire appears low.

Find the Correct Pressure

Use the placard on the driver’s door jamb, inside the fuel door, or in the owner’s manual. It lists the front and rear pressures for the vehicle. Tire sidewall markings include a maximum pressure, but that is not the normal target.

  • Measurements may be in pounds per square inch (PSI) or kilopascals (kPa). Use the unit shown on your gauge; many digital gauges display both.
  • If the placard gives separate front and rear values, compare each tire with the correct one.
  • The placard may reflect the vehicle’s size, trim, and load. If you are unsure which applies, check the manual rather than guessing.

Gather the Right Equipment

You need little more than a reliable tire-pressure gauge and access to every valve stem.

  • A digital gauge is easiest to read, but a quality mechanical gauge works too.
  • Use the gauge’s stated units and make sure it is properly calibrated and held straight.
  • A pen-type gauge can be useful, while an inaccurate gauge can send you chasing air.

Check the Tires

  • Park on level ground, set the parking brake, and switch off the engine. Keep the vehicle still while reading the gauge.
  • For the most accurate result, check cold tires, ideally after the vehicle has been parked for several hours. If you have just driven, the readings will be less precise.
  • Remove the valve cap and press the gauge firmly against the valve stem. With a digital gauge, listen for a steady reading and check for air escaping around the fitting.
  • Compare each reading with the recommended pressure for that position. Add air if a tire is below the target.
  • If a tire is above the target, let air out in small amounts and check it again. Never use the sidewall maximum as the goal. If the tire is warm, let it cool before bleeding it down.
  • Refit the caps, repeat for every tire, and make a final pass after any adjustment. If the vehicle has a spare, check it according to the owner’s manual because its required pressure may differ.

Know When to Check

  • Monthly, as part of routine vehicle maintenance.
